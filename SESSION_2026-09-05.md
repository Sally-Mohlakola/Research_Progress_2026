# Session Log — 5 September 2026

Neural diamond rendering. Everything marked **[measured]** is a number from a
render or checkpoint in this repository.

---

## Headline

One defect dominated everything else: **`Model_M`'s output was being zeroed at
render time**, so every neural render made before today was, in effect, an
analytic render. Fixing it, plus three smaller defects, moved the learned
term's share of the image from 0.05% to between 7% and 83% depending on
configuration — and made it possible to measure what aggregation actually does
to a gemstone, which is the project's research claim.

**The claim is now supported with a specific mechanism: aggregation conserves
energy and destroys contrast.** It is not a failure to carry non-local
specular transport; it is a failure to keep it concentrated.

---

## 1. Defects found and fixed

### 1.1 Activation mismatch — the big one

`Model_M.forward` is `exp(sequential(x))`. `MiModelWrapper` rebuilds the
render-time network by walking Linear and PReLU layers only, so the `exp` was
never carried across, and `eval.py` supplied a clamped ReLU instead. RDM
targets have median ~0.06, so pre-activations sit near ln(0.06):

**[measured]** on run_15's weights — 99.9% of pre-activations negative,
median −3.77. `relu(pre)` is exactly zero for 99.9% of queries.

Consequence: the neural lobe contributed **0.054%** of image energy. Fixed by
matching the training activation, `min(exp(min(x, 20)), clamp)`.

### 1.2 Gather aim point (plan §2.1)

`collect_rdm` aimed every ray at the stone's centre, so each direction
produced one ray through the middle and each facet was only ever entered
within the narrow cone it subtends from there.

**[measured]** incoming bins 41/64, with the entire grazing band
(theta_i 67.5–90°) empty — 0 of 16. Fixed by jittering the origin across the
disc perpendicular to the beam: **64/64**, outgoing cells non-zero
1179 → 21723. Rays that miss (31%, by design) are excluded from the histogram
counts via a new `active` argument to `compute_histogram_4d`.

### 1.3 Zero-bin filter (plan §2.2)

`train_models.py` dropped every bin with no energy, conflating "measured dark"
with "never sampled". Fixed using `count_i` as the mask. On run_09's data the
trainable set went 949 → 5248 bins. After §2.1 the point is largely moot —
gathers now come back 8192/8192 lit — but it matters at higher resolution.

### 1.4 Route A folding bug

The residual folded both `wi` and `wo` into the measured hemisphere. Since
70.5% of `rdm_m`'s outgoing energy sits on the normal's side, folding `wo`
aimed that energy **back into the solid**. Fixed by folding `wi` only:
residual share 4% → **11%**.

### 1.5 Environment

`config/__init__.py` sets `os.environ["DRJIT_LIBLLVM_PATH"]` to a hardcoded
Linux path, which breaks every script launched from a plain shell on this
machine. Worked around with a wrapper that initialises Mitsuba first; **not
fixed in the repo**.

## 2. What was built

**Route A, side-gated at k=1** (`bsdf/neural_bsdf.py`). `sample_entry` is a
smooth dispersive dielectric — Fresnel, Snell, TIR, hero-wavelength collapse —
run on rays arriving from outside; the neural residual handles rays arriving
from inside. **[measured]** it reproduces analytic short-path transport to
within 0.02% on both energy and peak. `--no_explicit_entry` disables it.

**Log-space loss** (`train_models.py`). `Model_M`'s pre-activation is
regressed against `log(y + floor)`, matching its `exp` output. Active by
default.

**Fourier encoding + wider net** (`neural/base_model.py`). 6 bands, 78 inputs,
128 wide. **[measured]** median relative fit error **0.555 → 0.047**, log-space
r 0.732 → 0.9994, torch/Dr.Jit agreement 1.4e-7. **Cannot be rendered** — see
blockers. Defaults reverted to width 21, no encoding; the architecture is now
recorded in `model_m_arch.json` and read back by `eval.py`.

**Depth-aware integrator** (`integrators/depth_aware.py`). Written; blocked.

## 3. What was measured

### Where a diamond's appearance lives **[measured]**

Analytic stone, whole-image energy by path depth:

| max_depth | energy | peak |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 24.3% | 0.0965 |
| 4 | 24.5% | 0.0965 |
| 8 | 86.8% | 0.5618 |
| 64 | 100% | 0.5618 |

**75% of the energy and all of the bright structure appear between bounce 4
and 8.** Below that you have polished glass.

### How much of a render is neural **[measured]** (run_18, spp 64)

| configuration | whole image | stone |
|---|---|---|
| Route A (`eval.py` default) | 9.5% | 4.0% |
| aggregate-only (`--no_explicit_entry`) | 77.9% | **83.1%** |

The share is a property of the configuration, not of model quality.

### What aggregation does **[measured]**

The RDM is energy-conserving — gathered albedos `rdm_t` 0.084, `rdm_r` 0.227,
`rdm_m` 0.680, total 0.991 — and the aggregate-only render returns 94% of the
reference's total light. But:

| | onto the stone | onto the ground |
|---|---|---|
| neural energy | **6.7%** | 93.3% |
| analytic reference | 36.6% | 63.4% |

and contrast collapses:

| | p99 / median on the stone |
|---|---|
| analytic reference | 22.91 |
| analytic GGX lobe alone (no network) | **24.16** |
| aggregate with the RDM on | **5.95** |

The non-neural lobe already has roughly the right contrast ratio; switching
the RDM on destroys it. At an 8x16 outgoing grid each bin spans ~22 x 22
degrees, so a few-degree exit lobe is smeared over ~500 square degrees.

### The decomposition works **[measured]**

Composite = analytic (depth ≤ 2) + the RDM's depth≥3 term, exact at k=2
because `rdm_m` is already conditioned on `depth >= 3`:

| | brightness | p99/median | top-1% |
|---|---|---|---|
| analytic reference | 1.000 | 22.20 | 15.4% |
| explicit only | 0.090 | 576.57 | 27.1% |
| aggregate only | 0.163 | 7.81 | 7.1% |
| **composite k=2** | **0.225** | **20.53** | **13.6%** |

Contrast within 8% of ground truth, where neither half is close.

### Three attributions tested and falsified **[measured]**

Each was a plausible reading of the images; ablation refuted all three.

* **The RDM makes the bright flashes.** No — turning `Model_M` off leaves the
  identical top-6 facets in identical order, stone correlation +0.999976, and
  the lit facets are 99.05% analytic.
* **The RDM makes the blue and gold casts.** No — `rdm_m`'s channel spread is
  exactly 0.000e+00 in every checkpoint, because the gather scene uses an
  achromatic `dielectric`. The casts are identical with the network off
  (R/B 1.337 vs 1.333). Fire is an entirely analytic capability.
* **The RDM captures crown reflection.** No — `rdm_r` is never loaded by
  `bsdf/rdm_sampler.py`. Yet the isolated neural contribution correlates with
  crown reflection at **+0.842**, higher than two genuine analytic crown
  reflections correlate with each other (+0.802).

That last case is the cleanest methodological result of the day: **+0.84
correlation with provably zero containment.** The common cause is facet
geometry — every BSDF assigns each facet a different local (wi, wo). It is a
concrete argument for why attribution must rest on same-configuration ablation
rather than on comparing renders.

### Unused gathered data **[measured]**

| | albedo | used at render time |
|---|---|---|
| `rdm_t` | 0.084 | sampling distribution only — value never evaluated |
| `rdm_r` | 0.227 | never loaded (deliberate: matches analytic Fresnel to 0.06%) |
| `rdm_m` | 0.680 | trained into `Model_M`, and in the sampler |

`Model_T` is trained for 3000 epochs every run and **never called** by the
BSDF. `rdm_t` is a genuine gap: depth-2 enter-and-exit transmission has no
analytic stand-in in either configuration.

## 4. Blockers

**`MiModelWrapper` cannot evaluate a useful network.** `matmul` materialises
an `out x in x lanes` outer product — 2.4 GB for the historical 21x6 layer,
**188 GB** for 128x78. An accumulating rewrite scaled better but segfaulted at
render width. The fix is `drjit.nn` / `CoopVec`, which Dr.Jit 1.5 ships for
exactly this. **This blocks the better architecture and everything downstream
of it.**

**The custom integrator segfaults** deterministically on this build, at every
resolution, with recording on or off. Three real bugs were fixed along the way
(`scene.eval_environment` does not exist in 3.9, `dr.eq` is gone, shape
detection was picking a light panel); after those it fails with no Python-level
error.

**Memory pressure.** Two background runs were killed by the OS today.

## 6. What to do next

### First: time something. One afternoon.

Nothing in this project has ever been timed, and efficiency is the entire
justification for a neural appearance model. Time the analytic render at
max_depth 2, 4, 8 and 64, and the neural configurations. Until that exists you
have a fidelity result with no efficiency claim, and no way to say whether the
decomposition saves anything at all. `create_scene` hardcodes `max_depth: 64`
at `eval.py:412`, so that needs exposing first.

### Second: measure entry–exit distance. A few lines.

`RESIDUAL_DECOMPOSITION_PLAN.md` puts "median 1.23 radii" in the thesis
statement and in three figure captions, but `entry_point` / `exit_point` are
recorded nowhere in the code and the plan lists the measurement as unstarted.
`trace_path` already holds `si_first` and `rays_final`, so it is a small
change.

This matters more than its size. If light enters at one point and leaves 1.23
radii away, then a surface BSDF cannot represent that transport *by
definition* — an argument immune to both objections that threaten everything
else ("your network was too small", "your bins were too coarse"). It is the
strongest available form of the claim, and the number is currently a
placeholder.

### Third: port `MiModelWrapper` to `drjit.nn` (CoopVec).

This unblocks the encoded 128-wide network, which fits the RDM twelve times
better. Until it exists, every neural measurement is a floor rather than the
method's ceiling, and the resolution sweep cannot be interpreted — a plateau
would be the network saturating rather than aggregation failing.

### Then, in order of value

1. **The "analytic prior" formulation.** Train `Model_M` as a correction to a
   known analytic base — the base as an input feature, or inside the loss —
   rather than fitting `rdm_m` in isolation. This directly targets the measured
   failure: the RDM currently flattens a base that already had the right
   contrast, because it has no idea the base exists.
2. **Angular resolution sweep**, outgoing axis only, incoming held at 8x16.
   Splitting those axes needs a change at `utils/rdm.py:230-261`, where one
   parameter pair currently drives both. 32x64 outgoing costs ~221M rays.
3. **Facet conditioning** (6 → 10 inputs). Gives the representation a coarse
   positional coordinate, and tests the 6D hypothesis cheaply.
4. **Resolve `Model_T`** — wire it in or delete it. Trained every run, never
   called, and it will read as an unfinished implementation in a viva.
5. **Use `rdm_t`**, or state why not. 8% of the gathered albedo, already
   collected, currently absent from every render.

### On the thesis framing

The diagnosis is the contribution, and it is nearly complete. The decomposition
is an implemented and measured response to it. The 6D non-local representation
is the conclusion the diagnosis argues for — worth proposing with the
feasibility numbers, not worth building in the time available: as a histogram
it needs 56 billion rays for both spatial and angular resolution, and as a
learned model it needs a learned sampler and a working custom integrator.

Two claims to avoid, both of which today's measurements would contradict:
that the neural model produces the stone's fire, and that it produces the
facet shading. At facet-tone prediction it scores +0.151 against a non-neural
lobe's +0.266.
